*Module 19 · Part V — Practice*

A guardrail you haven't measured is a guardrail you don't have. Evaluation is what separates "we
added a classifier" from "we know it catches 90% of attacks and over-refuses 2% of real users."
And it always comes down to **two numbers read together** — catch rate and false-alarm rate.

## The frontier you're aiming for

Plot any guardrail by attack-success (lower better) and over-refusal (lower better). The goal is
the bottom-left corner — and the only way to get there is to escalate rather than refuse (M12).

## The toolkit

- **Harm / jailbreak benchmarks:** HarmBench, JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT — standardized attack
  sets that give you a comparable attack-success rate.
- **Agent benchmarks:** AgentDojo and similar — injection and tool-misuse against agents (M8).
- **Over-refusal sets:** XSTest and friends — benign prompts that look risky, to measure the cost
  of your guardrails on real users.
- **Runners:** garak, PyRIT, promptfoo — automate running these against your system in CI.
- **Scale:** providers run large automated red-teaming (OpenAI reported 700k+ GPU-hours; Anthropic
  1,700+ human hours) and keep running it during deployment, because new jailbreaks never stop.

## Rules of engagement

Red-teaming an LLM system is still security testing: scope it, get authorization, and keep it to
systems you own or are permitted to test. Use responsible disclosure and provider bug-bounty
programs for findings against a provider's model rather than publishing working attacks.
