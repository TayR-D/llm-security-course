*Module 18 · Part IV — Guardrails compared*

You don't have to be a frontier lab to put guardrails on an LLM app. There's a whole ecosystem of
**deployable** safeguards — open guard models, orchestration frameworks, and cloud services — that
you wrap around any model, including a self-hosted open-weight one. This is the most directly
useful module for your own platform.

- **Open guard models** (classifiers you run): Llama Guard 4, Prompt Guard 2, Qwen3Guard,
  gpt-oss-safeguard, ShieldGemma, Granite Guardian, Nemotron safety guards.
- **Frameworks** (wire guards into a pipeline): LlamaFirewall (incl. AlignmentCheck for agents),
  NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, LLM Guard.
- **Cloud services** (managed): Amazon Bedrock Guardrails, Azure AI Content Safety with Prompt
  Shields, Google Model Armor.

## Choosing one isn't obvious

A 2026 benchmark of 14 open guard models found two things worth internalizing: **model size does
not predict safety** (a 4B guard out-recalled a 20B one), and **precision-optimized guards can
miss most unsafe content** — high precision with low recall looks great until you realize it's
waving through the attacks. Pick a guard and set its strictness; watch the confusion matrix and
the precision/recall tension.

## How to actually deploy this

The pattern that works (and the one your capstone will use):

1. **Screen input** with a prompt/injection guard (Prompt Guard 2 / Qwen3Guard) — including on
   tool and RAG content, not just the user turn (M8).
2. **Screen output** with a content guard (Llama Guard 4 / ShieldGemma / Granite), judged in the
   context of the input where the tool allows it.
3. **Wrap agents** with a framework (LlamaFirewall) for trajectory/alignment checks.
4. **Measure** before you trust it: run your own test set (M19) and watch recall, not just
   precision. A guard that only catches the plain cases is theater (M2).

:::tip[For a self-hosted open-weight model]
Because your model has no trained-in refusal to rely on (M11), these external guards plus
identity and containment (M13, M14) are most of your safety. Layer them; don't bet on one.
:::

## Check yourself

1. In the playground, find a guard+strictness with high precision but poor recall. Why is that
   combination dangerous for a security platform?
2. Why screen *tool output* with the same guard you use on user input?
3. Which deployable layer addresses agent *actions* rather than message *content*?
