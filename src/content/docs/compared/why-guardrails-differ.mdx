*Module 17 · Part IV — Guardrails compared*

Ask three frontier models the same borderline question and you can get three different
responses: one answers, one refuses, one quietly routes you to a weaker model. That isn't
randomness. It's because **guardrails live in different places for different providers**,
and increasingly depend on *who is asking*.

This module makes the differences concrete. The matrix below compares three providers
across eight dimensions — every cell links to a primary source, carries an evidence tag,
and shows when it was last checked.

## The pattern to notice

Read down the **"Response to a flag"** row. The big 2026 shift is away from a hard refusal
toward **rerouting**: a flagged request is handed to a less capable model rather than
bounced. That's what lets the same account get help with vulnerability *discovery* but a
downgraded answer for exploit *development* — the capability is gated, not the user.

Then read the **"Access tiers"** row. All three providers now reserve their strongest
cyber capability for *vetted defenders* through a named program. The guardrail is no longer
only "what is being asked" but "who is asking, and have they been verified."

## Case study: one model, two safeguard sets

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 are the **same underlying model** with
different safeguards — Mythos is the vetted-access build with cyber and life-science
restrictions relaxed. Compare that to open weights, where refusal can be removed from the
model itself, and the whole "trained refusal" layer simply doesn't exist for a determined
user. Same capability, four different control points depending on the release.
