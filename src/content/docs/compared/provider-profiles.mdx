*Module 16 · Part IV — Guardrails compared*

Before comparing providers cell-by-cell (M17), read each one whole. This module puts every
provider on the **same template** so you can see the shape of its approach: what it trains in,
what it screens at runtime, how it gates access, which framework governs it, and what it changed
after an incident.

The biggest fault line is **closed API vs open weight**. A closed-API provider stacks safeguards
it controls end-to-end; an open-weight provider ships you a toolkit and the responsibility —
which is the world your self-hosted platform lives in.

## How to read a profile

Three questions get you most of the way when you assess any provider (or your own stack):

1. **Where do the safeguards live?** In the weights (travels everywhere, can't be patched fast),
   in runtime classifiers (patchable, adds latency/cost), in the platform (identity, enforcement),
   or nowhere that survives a download (open weights).
2. **What happens on a flag?** Hard refuse, safe-complete, or reroute — and who can lift the
   restriction (access tiers).
3. **What does the framework require, and who checks it?** The governing framework (M15) and any
   external testing.

## Check yourself

1. Compare Anthropic and Meta on "Runtime classifiers." Why is one a single sentence about the
   base model and the other a list of add-on tools?
2. For the open-weight profiles, which template fields are essentially "whatever the deployer
   does"? What does that tell you about your capstone's responsibilities?
3. Which provider is most *developer-configurable* at runtime, and what's the security
   trade-off of that flexibility?
