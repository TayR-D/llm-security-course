Before cataloguing attacks, get the shared maps. Four reference frameworks organize this
field, and knowing which one owns which risk keeps a security conversation precise:

- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications** — risks where the model is a *component* in an app
  (prompt injection, output handling, disclosure).
- **OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications** — risks once the model *acts*: tools, memory,
  autonomy (goal hijack, tool misuse, memory poisoning).
- **MITRE ATLAS** — an adversary-tactics knowledge base for ML systems, in the style of
  ATT&CK.
- **NIST AI RMF / adversarial ML taxonomy** — governance and a standard vocabulary for
  attack classes.

## What the 2026 re-rank tells you

The 2026 OWASP LLM list is the first weighted by real incident data, not just a practitioner
vote. The movements are a signal about where harm is actually landing. Explore them below.

## Reading the signal

- **Excessive Agency's jump to #3** is the headline: agents acting on real systems are now
  where damage concentrates. The whole of Part III's agentic defenses (M14) follow from this.
- **Prompt Injection stays #1 despite few public incidents.** OWASP calls this a "defense
  effect" — teams spend heavily to keep it rare, which hides how dangerous it remains.
- **The hard boundary:** the LLM Top 10 covers the model-as-component. The moment it has
  tools, memory and authority to act, risk moves to the Agentic Top 10. The course mirrors
  that split: Part II attacks here, agentic defense in M14.

:::tip[Use it as a checklist]
These lists are most useful as a shared checklist between you and a team. When you threat-model
your own system (M1, the capstone), walk each entry and ask "where does this live in my
architecture?" (M5).
:::

## Check yourself

1. Why does weighting by incident data push *Misinformation* up but leave *Prompt Injection*
   at #1 despite few incidents?
2. A risk involves an agent sending money based on a poisoned document. Which framework owns
   it — LLM Top 10 or Agentic Top 10? Why?
3. Name one thing MITRE ATLAS gives you that the OWASP lists don't.
