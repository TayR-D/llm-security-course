*Module 9 · Part II — The attack landscape*

Not every attack goes through the prompt. Some target the model's weights, its training data,
or its retrieval pipeline — often before the model ever serves a request. These are quieter
and harder to catch, because the damage is baked in upstream.

- **Data poisoning & backdoors** — corrupt training or fine-tuning data so the model learns a
  hidden trigger or a skewed behavior. The 2026 OWASP entry now explicitly includes
  *fine-tuning subversion* (M6).
- **Training-data extraction** — coax memorized secrets or personal data back out of the
  model.
- **Model extraction & distillation** — query a model at scale to train a cheaper copy. A
  distilled copy can ship *without the original's safeguards*, which is why providers now treat
  distillation as a safety risk and add anti-distillation measures (M3, M13).
- **Weight theft** — the top prize for a resourced actor; what frontier security levels are
  built to defend (M15).
- **Supply chain** — compromised base models, adapters, datasets, or unsafe model files
  (e.g. pickle-based formats that execute code on load).
- **RAG / vector-store poisoning** — add a chunk to a knowledge base so it gets retrieved for a
  target query and injects once in context.

## Why these are hard

Prompt-time attacks are at least *observable* at inference. These upstream threats are not:
a backdoor or a poisoned chunk sits dormant until its trigger. Defenses are mostly about
**provenance and isolation**, not runtime classifiers:

- trusted, verified ingestion for training data and RAG sources;
- per-tenant isolation so one tenant can't poison another's retrieval (relevant to your
  multi-tenant capstone);
- signed, safe model formats and dependency pinning;
- screening retrieved content before use, as a backstop.
