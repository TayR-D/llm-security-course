Runtime guardrails are the layers you can update without retraining. They screen traffic as it
flows, and they're where most of the 2026 innovation happened. The central design problem is a
trade-off you can't escape: catch more attacks, or refuse fewer benign users — a single
threshold can't do both.

- **Input / output / exchange classifiers.** Input classifiers screen the request (including
  tool/RAG content); output classifiers screen the answer. The key 2026 move was the
  **exchange** classifier — judging the output *in the context of its input* — which is what
  catches reconstruction and output-obfuscation attacks that beat input- or output-only
  screening (M7).
- **Constitution-trained classifiers.** The same written policy that aligns the model trains
  the classifier, so they agree on what's disallowed.
- **Cascades.** A cheap first stage screens everything and *escalates* flagged traffic to an
  expensive second stage instead of refusing — cutting both over-refusal and compute.
- **Activation probes / classifiers.** Read the model's internal activations ("gut feeling")
  rather than surface text; harder to fool with obfuscation, and cheap enough to run on all
  traffic.
- **Chain-of-thought monitoring.** Watch the reasoning trace for trouble — useful, but fragile:
  a model can learn to hide intent from its own CoT (M14).

## The numbers that make this real

Anthropic's next-generation **Constitutional Classifiers++** are the clearest public example of
the cascade + activation-probe design: a linear probe screens all traffic and escalates flagged
exchanges, reaching a **0.05% refusal rate on harmless queries** (an 87% drop from the first
generation) at roughly **1% added compute**, with no universal jailbreak found across 1,700+
hours of red-teaming
([Anthropic, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers)).
OpenAI's GPT-5.6 ships **activation classifiers that can halt generation mid-stream**
([GPT-5.6 system card, Jul 2026](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6)).
