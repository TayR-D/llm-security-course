Some of the most important safeguards aren't in the model or its classifiers at all — they're in
the *platform*: who you are, what you're allowed, and what happens when you cross a line. This
is the layer that turned "what is being asked" into "who is asking, and have they been verified."

- **Usage policies + enforcement ladders.** The written rules, plus graduated consequences
  (warnings, rate limits, suspension).
- **Trusted-access programs.** Verified defenders unlock capability the general model withholds:
  Anthropic's Cyber Verification Program and Life Sciences Verification Program, OpenAI's Trusted
  Access for Cyber, Google's Fairwind Program.
- **Fallback routing.** Flagged requests are *rerouted* to a weaker model rather than refused —
  the single biggest lever on over-refusal (M12).
- **Per-end-user safety identifiers.** Let a provider act on one abusive user instead of an
  entire organization.
- **Anti-distillation.** Measures that make it harder to extract a model's capabilities into an
  unguarded copy (M9).
- **Misuse detection with privacy.** Systems like Enterprise Frontier Safeguards detect misuse
  while keeping zero-data-retention privacy — review happens on the customer's own
  infrastructure.

## Why this is the layer that scales

Model- and classifier-level safeguards treat every request the same. The access layer is what
lets a provider say "yes" to a verified hospital security team and "rerouted" to an anonymous
account for the *identical* prompt — the only way to serve dual-use work without opening the
capability to everyone. It's also the layer most relevant to *your* platform: on a self-hosted,
multi-tenant deployment with an unguarded model, tenant identity and enforcement are much of the
safety you have (the capstone).
